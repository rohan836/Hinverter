# Hinverter

DIY **12 V → 230 V SPWM full-bridge inverter** firmware for a Raspberry Pi Pico
(RP2040), written in MicroPython.

Driver chain:

```
Pico (RP2040)  ──SPWM+dead-time──▶  PC123 optocouplers ──▶  BD139/BD140 totem poles
      │                                                      │  (2× per switch)
      │                                              8× IRF540 MOSFETs ──▶ 12→230 V
      │                                                      │        transformer
      ▼                                                      ▼
  SSD1306-mode OLED    ACS712 #1 (output)  ACS712 #2 (battery)   MLX90614
```

> **Status: UNVERIFIED ON HARDWARE.** This firmware has been written and
> syntax-checked, and its pure logic is unit-tested on a PC, but it has **not
> been run on your bench**. Do not power the H-bridge with real loads until
> each stage has been verified individually (see "Bench commissioning").
> Electronics with batteries can start fires. Read the safety section first.

---

## Features

- **Bipolar SPWM, 50 Hz (selectable 60 Hz)** generated by a Pico **PIO
  state machine with hardware-enforced 1.5 µs dead-time** between the
  high-side and low-side MOSFETs of each H-bridge leg (no hardware-PWM
  "complementary output" shoot-through).
- **1.3" 128×64 OLED** (I2C, SSD1306-compatible mode): Bruce-firmware-style UI — bordered screens,
  status bar with battery icon, launcher home pages (Inverter / Battery /
  Thermal), inverted selected rows, blinking fault latch screen, boot splash.
  Verified on the included unit: it answers to the SSD1306 init sequence
  (0x8D charge pump + 0x20 page mode); the SH1106 sequence leaves it dark.
  `config.OLED_DRIVER` selects the controller; the driver supports both.
- **Encoder UI** (HW-040): rotate = scroll/select, short press = select/edit,
  long press (≥1.5 s) = RUN/STOP toggle / exit-edit.
- **Feedback + protection**, latched until acknowledge:
  - Over-current (rolling RMS + fast peak detect; ACS712 #1)
  - Over-temperature (MLX90614 non-contact IR sensor on the heatsink)
  - Low battery cutoff (33k/10k divider on ADC2 — ACS712 cannot measure volts)
- **Settings persist** to `settings.json` in flash.
- **Watchdog** (8 s): on firmware hang, MCU resets and all gate pins revert to
  inputs (MOSFETs off).
- **Output never auto-starts.** Boot state is STOP, all gates LOW.

## Wiring & hardware notes (read this — it will save your MOSFETs)

1. **High-side N-channel drive is marginal at 12 V.** An IRF540 source-follower
   tops out around Vgs ≈ 8 V minus drop; the BD139/BD140 totem pole can only
   pull the gate to ~12 V minus one Vbe. Verify Q1/Q3 actually enhance with a
   dummy load **before** connecting the transformer. If they don't, the fix is
   hardware (bootstrap or a high-side driver such as IR2184) — no firmware
   change can fix a transistor that never turns on.
2. **Opto-isolation vs. measurement.** The PC123 gate side may be isolated,
   but **ACS712 and the battery divider must share ground with the Pico ADC**.
   Fully isolating everything breaks current/voltage readings.
3. **ACS712 power at 3.3 V, not 5 V.** The module output is ratiometric
   (zero = Vcc/2). At 5 V supply its swing exceeds the Pico ADC range above
   ~8 A. At 3.3 V: zero ≈ 1.65 V, sensitivity ≈ 66 mV/A, full-scale ≈ ±25 A,
   safe on GP26/GP27. (If you keep modules at 5 V anyway, you *must* add a
   divider and recalibrate `COUNTS_PER_AMP`.)
4. **Battery voltage divider.** 33 kΩ (top, from +12 V) → GP28 ← 10 kΩ (bottom,
   to GND). Gain = 4.3; 14.4 V charging → ≈ 3.0 V at the ADC. Never feed raw
   12 V into a Pico pin.
5. **I2C pull-ups must go to 3.3 V.** Many OLED/MLX modules pull SDA/SCL up to
   5 V; remove or re-tie those resistors or you will slowly kill the Pico pins.
6. **Gate hygiene.** Keep the 8× 10 kΩ gate→source resistors and 8× 220 Ω
   series resistors. With ~50–100 mA gate current from BD139/BD140, the 220 Ω
   limits di/dt; the 10 kΩ guarantee off-state at power-up.
7. **Never energize the H-bridge without the transformer, LC filter and a load.**
   An open-circuit inductive primary rings hard at turn-off.
8. **Common ground between Pico and power stage is required** even with the
   opto-isolated gate path (see #2). Star-ground power returns away from the
   ADC/I2C wiring.

## Flashing

1. Flash MicroPython onto the Pico with `RPI_PICO-20260824-v1.29.0.uf2`
   (hold BOOTSEL, plug in USB, drag the .uf2 into the `RPI-RP2` drive).
2. Copy all `*.py` files and `settings.json` to the Pico root with **Thonny**
   (install to device) or `mpremote cp`:
   ```
   mpremote cp inv_math.py config.py settings.py sensors.py spwm.py protect.py \
               encoder.py oled.py screens.py main.py settings.json :
   ```
3. Reset the Pico; the OLED boots to the home screen in STOP state.

`tests/` and `README.md` do not need to be copied to the device.

## Operation

The UI is a **Bruce-firmware-style** design adapted to a monochrome 128×64
OLED (SSD1306 mode): 1-px rounded-rectangle border on every screen, a top status bar
(`HINV` left, battery icon + volts right), black background with inverted
(white-bar/black-text) accents standing in for Bruce's RGB565 colors.
Monochrome OLED has no color/grayscale, so "accents" are inverted pixels.

At boot a splash is shown (logo + sine plot, ~1.2 s).

### Home pages (Bruce launcher model)
Rotate switches pages; short press → Settings; long press (≥1.5 s) → RUN/STOP.

**Inverter page** — big pixel bolt icon, `<` `>` chevrons, RUN shown as a
full-width inverted bar (STOP = plain text), caption shows output setting:
```
 HINV          12.6V [||--]
 ────────────────────────
 <   ⚡⚡      >
     STOP      ← or inverted RUN bar
  Out:230V D:100%
```

**Battery page** — battery icon whose fill tracks an estimated rested
lead-acid percentage (crude scale, not a fuel gauge), volts + amps.

**Thermal page** — thermometer icon, heatsink temp, and a fill bar against
the 70 °C (configurable) trip limit.

### Settings screen (Bruce Config model)
Centered option rows; the selected row is full-width inverted.
Rotate = select, short press = edit, long press = back to home.
Editing: rotate = adjust, short press = save, long press = cancel.

| Setting | Range | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output V | 100–240 (step 5) | 230 | modulation dial, not closed-loop |
| Frequency | 50 / 60 Hz | 50 | snaps to the two |
| I Limit A | 0.5–20 A | 8.0 | RMS trip; 1.5× = instant peak trip |
| OverTemp C | 40–100 °C | 70 | MLX90614 object temperature |
| LowBat V | 9.0–12.6 V | 10.8 | lead-acid discharge floor |

Persisted to `settings.json`; invalid/corrupt files fall back to safe defaults.

### Faults (all latched until acknowledged)

| Fault | Trigger |
|---|---|
| `OVERCURRENT` | burst RMS > limit for 5 rounds, or any burst peak > 1.5× limit |
| `OVERTEMP` | MLX90614 object temp > OverTemp C |
| `LOW BATTERY` | battery voltage (divider) < LowBat V |

Fault state = all gate pins LOW (SMs halted): no MOSFET conducting,
transformer primary open-circuit — the safest latch for this bridge.

## Calibration

1. **ACS712 zero offset** — measured automatically at power-up (inverter off).
   Keep the output unloaded for the first ~0.5 s after boot. For better
   accuracy, call `out_i.zero_calibrate()` / `bat_i.zero_calibrate()` after
   the power stage has fully switched off.
2. **Counts per amp** — `config.COUNTS_PER_AMP` assumes 3.3 V-powered
   ACS712-20A (66 mV/A). Verify with a known load: read
   `ACS712.counts_to_amps(raw)` at a measured 5 A and adjust the constant.
3. **Battery divider gain** — default 4.3 (33k/10k). Measure GP28 voltage
   with a DMM against battery voltage and adjust `config.BATTERY_DIVIDER_GAIN`.

## Bench commissioning (do this in order)

1. Pico + OLED only; verify UI, encoder, fault screens.
2. Add ACS712s → verify current readings with a clampmeter reference.
3. Add MLX90614 → verify heatsink temp; point it at a known warm object.
4. Add gate drivers **with no transformer connected** → scope GP10–GP13;
   confirm zero overlap between high and low within each leg (dead-time ≈ 1.5 µs).
5. Connect transformer, **lamp load only** → start at Output V = 150, raise
   toward 230V while monitoring battery current and heatsink temp.
6. Verify fault latch: short a low-resistance load momentarily (through a fuse),
   or raise OverTemp C low; confirm latch + acknowledge works.
7. Only then connect real loads.

## Design decisions & honest limitations

- **SPWM frequency**: 20 kHz carrier; duty updated at 5 kHz from a sine LUT
  (100 points per mains cycle). The transformer + LC filter must supply the
  low-pass pole; bipolar SPWM pushes the fundamental fully through the
  primary, with carrier ripple rejected by the filter.
- **No closed-loop voltage regulation.** Output volts = modulation index ×
  battery voltage × transformer ratio, unloaded. Sagging battery → sagging
  output; closed-loop control would need output voltage sensing, which is not
  in the BOM.
- **RMS is approximated** from a ~64-sample burst (~1 mains cycle) per meter
  read, plus EMA. Good enough for protection and rough display, not a
  billing-grade meter.
- **Output RMS display uses the *setting*, not a measurement.** "Duty" shows
  the live modulation percentage.
- **The encoder long-press is overloaded**: long press toggles RUN/STOP on
  the home screen and cancels/exits elsewhere; voltage editing lives on the
  Settings screen because the gesture was claimed twice.

## Safety

- **Never** open the inverter or the battery while the H-bridge is enabled.
- Lead-acid 12 V batteries can source hundreds of amps into a short. A failed
  totem pole (both IRF540s on) makes a direct short through the battery
  internal resistance. Use a fuse (e.g. 30 A automotive) in series with the
  battery lead.
- Heatsinks get hot. MLX90614 trip is the last line of defence, not a substitute
  for a properly sized heatsink and airflow.
- This project is not certified for mains connection; 230 V can kill. Keep the
  transformer, filter and wiring enclosed and insulated.
- If anything smells hot or the OLED glitches, long-press is not your only
  stop button: **the battery fuse is**.
